TL;DR

I led the design of a scalable Digital 1003 co-borrower experience that helped applicants and loan officers add, organize, and manage borrower pairs more clearly within the mortgage application flow.

The Problem

The existing borrower flow did not fully support the complexity of mortgage applications involving multiple borrowers. Loan officers and applicants needed a clearer way to add co-borrowers, organize borrower relationships, and manage borrower pairs without creating confusion or compliance risk.

The Details

Role: Senior UX Designer

Timeline: 3 months

Product: Digital 1003 Mortgage Application

Platform: Web (Internal Tool)

Impact / Efficiency

By simplifying complex multi-borrower workflows, the redesign supported a more efficient application experience. Post-launch reporting showed stronger application productivity, lock volume, and lead-to-lock conversion across the broader Origins platform.

A streamlined experience with measurable outcomes

The Managing Borrowers redesign reduced complexity within multi-borrower applications. Following the larger Origins rollout, operational reporting showed stronger application productivity, lock activity, and conversion across the lending funnel.

UX GOALS

Goals That Guided the Redesign:

My goal was to improve the overall user experience by making the platform more intuitive, efficient, and scalable. I focused on creating clearer workflows, stronger visual hierarchy, and a structure that could better support future Digital 1003 needs.

  1. Make adding co-borrowers easier
  2. Clarify borrower relationships
  3. Reduce loan officer confusion
  4. Support compliance and AUS needs
  5. Create a scalable flow

UX APPROACH

Design Strategy:

During early exploration, I tested different ways to represent borrower relationships, including list-based structures, grouped cards, and step-based flows. The design needed to make complex borrower logic feel simple without hiding important details from loan officers.

Step 1: Mapped borrower flows from entry point to review

Step 2: Defined how co-borrowers would be added and grouped

Step 3: Designed borrower pair cards to improve visibility

Step 4: Created clear hierarchy between primary borrower, co-borrower, and pair details

Step 5: Reduced cognitive load by breaking complex borrower logic into manageable steps

Step 6: Partnered with product and engineering to validate edge cases

Final Designs

Takeaways

Final thoughts and takeaways

The next phase would focus on validating the experience with loan officers and operations teams using realistic borrower scenarios. I would measure task completion, setup errors, and the time required to add co-borrowers, create borrower pairs, and assign vesting types. These findings would help identify opportunities to refine the workflow before expanding it across the broader digital 1003 application.

Next Steps

I would explore quick actions for editing or reorganizing borrower pairs. I would also introduce learning resources or tooltips to help new users understand vesting types and complex borrower structures without interrupting the primary workflow.
